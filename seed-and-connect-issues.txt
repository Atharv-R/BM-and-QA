seed_and_connect strategy — open issues (April 2026)
=====================================================

Problem: Some classes get stuck at 1-2 nodes because their seed lands on a
hidden node whose hidden-subgraph neighbors all get claimed by earlier classes.
The frontier dries up and the chain can't grow.

Root cause: The greedy seed selection picks the globally best 2-hop-coverage
node, but doesn't account for whether that node has enough hidden neighbors
to sustain growth. With 10 classes competing for frontier nodes round-robin,
early classes can wall off later seeds.

What we tried:
1. Original: multi-seed placement + Steiner tree connection.
   Failed because seeds spread across disconnected regions of the hidden
   subgraph; Steiner trees consumed hundreds of connector nodes or couldn't
   find paths at all (Mehlhorn KeyError, then node-disjointness starvation).

2. Single-seed + connected growth with 2-hop pixel coverage metric.
   Fixed connectivity (chains always connected by construction) but some
   classes still get stuck at 1-2 nodes when their seed's frontier is
   entirely consumed by other classes.

3. Restricting to giant connected component of hidden subgraph.
   Didn't help — the hidden subgraph is already one giant component (4016
   nodes, all connected). The issue is local frontier exhaustion, not
   global disconnection.

4. Counting 2-hop coverage only through hidden intermediaries (h→hidden→pixel).
   Improved seed placement (seeds now have hidden neighbors) but still 2
   classes stuck at 2 nodes.

Ideas not yet tried:
- Add a tie-breaking / penalty term for hidden-degree (ensure seeds have
  ample room to grow, not just high coverage).
- Reserve frontier capacity: before seeding, estimate how many growth steps
  each seed can sustain and penalize low-capacity seeds.
- Backtracking: if a class gets stuck, steal a neighbor from a larger chain
  or re-seed entirely.
- Two-phase: first run connected_greedy to guarantee full chains, then
  measure 2-hop coverage as a quality metric for comparison.
